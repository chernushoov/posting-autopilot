# Top 5 Agency Demo Briefs

## Lead 1: Urban Recruits

### Profile
Small tech recruitment agency, Tel Aviv, 1–10 employees, active Facebook page, multilingual roles.

### Demo Angle
**Lead with:** Speed. Small team = every hour matters. "Your 3 recruiters each save 1–2 hours a day."
**Pain to emphasize:** Writing individual posts in Hebrew takes time. Tracking across groups is manual/nonexistent.
**Show in demo:** Generate a Hebrew post for a realistic tech role (e.g., "Full Stack Developer — Tel Aviv"). Show the tone switch from Professional to Casual. Show group selection filtered by "Tech + Tel Aviv."

### Most Likely Objection
"We're small, we manage fine with copy-paste."
**Counter:** "Copy-paste works, but you don't know which groups actually bring CVs. After 1 week with us, you will. And the AI writes better posts than templates — every time."

### What NOT to Say
- Don't call them "small" — say "focused" or "lean."
- Don't imply they need to change their whole process.

### Pilot Close Line
```
"Let's do a quick test. I'll set you up in 15 minutes, we'll post one real vacancy together, and in a week you'll have data on which groups work. Free, no commitment. When's good?"
```

## Lead 2: Yitron Enoshi

### Profile
Industrial, supply chain, logistics recruitment. National. Has WhatsApp on website. Small–medium.

### Demo Angle
**Lead with:** Group intelligence. Blue collar groups are hit-or-miss — some are dead, some are gold. "You'll know which groups work for warehouse/logistics roles within a week."
**Pain to emphasize:** Industrial roles need volume. Posting to 30 groups manually for each role is brutal.
**Show in demo:** Generate a post for a blue collar role (e.g., "עובד מחסן — באר שבע"). Show group directory filtered by "Blue Collar + South." Show the posting queue speed.

### Most Likely Objection
"Our candidates don't come from Facebook."
**Counter:** "Which channels are working for you? [Listen.] Many agencies in your sector find that Facebook groups for blue collar roles have huge reach — especially in the periphery. The pilot is free — worth testing for 2 weeks to see the data."

### What NOT to Say
- Don't assume they know Facebook groups well — they may be newer to this channel.
- Don't oversell AI sophistication — keep it practical.

### Pilot Close Line
```
"Let's test one thing: I'll load 15 industrial/logistics groups for you, you post one role, and we'll see what comes back. If it doesn't work, you've lost nothing. When can we do 15 minutes together?"
```

## Lead 3: Oman Agencies

### Profile
Uses Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, WhatsApp for recruitment. Mixed sectors. Israel placements.

### Demo Angle
**Lead with:** Efficiency across channels. "You're already on Facebook — we make the Facebook group part 5x faster so you have more time for the other channels."
**Pain to emphasize:** Multi-channel agencies are stretched thin. The time they spend on manual FB group posting could go to LinkedIn, Instagram, or candidate calls.
**Show in demo:** Generate a post, show group selection, emphasize the speed of the queue (15 seconds per group). Calculate: "20 groups × 15 seconds = 5 minutes instead of 40 minutes."

### Most Likely Objection
"We already manage our social channels well."
**Counter:** "I can see that — your presence is strong. The question is: do you know which Facebook groups actually produce candidates vs. which are a waste of time? That's what we track. Most agencies can't answer that question."

### What NOT to Say
- Don't suggest replacing their other channels. Position as a layer on top of what they already do.
- Don't criticize their current social strategy.

### Pilot Close Line
```
"You're already doing the work — we just make the Facebook group piece faster and trackable. 14 days free, I'll set it up. When can we start?"
```

## Lead 4: Medulla

### Profile
Hi-tech recruitment, Tel Aviv, small. Two Facebook pages. Social-media-forward.

### Demo Angle
**Lead with:** Post quality. They already invest in social media — they care about how their brand looks. "AI generates posts that are better than templates — every time, every tone."
**Pain to emphasize:** Even social-savvy agencies copy-paste the same post everywhere. AI generates unique, tailored posts per audience.
**Show in demo:** Generate a post for a senior tech role. Show 4 tone variations (Professional, Casual, Urgent, Young Audience). Emphasize that each can target a different group type.

### Most Likely Objection
"We already have good content/branding."
**Counter:** "Your content is strong — I can see from your pages. This isn't about replacing your brand voice. It's about doing the group posting piece faster — the part that's repetitive. And the tracking will show you which groups deserve your best content."

### What NOT to Say
- Don't imply their current posts are bad.
- Don't focus on the "manual posting" angle — they may not see it as a pain. Focus on tracking and content variation.

### Pilot Close Line
```
"You're clearly invested in how you present on social. Let's add group-level tracking so you know where that investment pays off. 14 days, free. Can we set up this week?"
```

## Lead 5: Nisha Group

### Profile
Hi-tech, biotech, finance. 25+ years established. Posts on social media. Likely 15–40 staff.

### Demo Angle
**Lead with:** Data and professionalism. Established firm = they value process, systems, and data. "After 25 years, you know the industry. Now you'll know which Facebook groups work — with data."
**Pain to emphasize:** Large established firms have recruiters posting manually without centralized tracking. Nobody knows the full picture of group performance.
**Show in demo:** Show the results tracking view. Show group performance comparison ("Group A: 12 CVs from 5 posts. Group B: 1 CV from 5 posts."). Emphasize the team visibility angle.

### Most Likely Objection
"We're too big/established to adopt a new tool right now."
**Counter:** "Totally understand — you have systems. This doesn't replace anything. It adds a layer on top of your Facebook group posting. One recruiter tries it for 14 days. If it saves time and shows which groups work, you roll it out. If not, you stop. Zero disruption."

### What NOT to Say
- Don't talk down to them — they have 25 years of experience.
- Don't pitch urgency or ban risk strongly — they're more risk-averse by nature. Lead with data and efficiency.
- Don't say "startup" or "we just launched" — emphasize the product's capability, not your stage.

### Pilot Close Line
```
"Here's what I'd suggest: one recruiter, one vacancy type, 14 days. At the end you'll have group performance data you've never had before. No disruption to your existing process. Can we do a 15-minute setup this week?"
```

## Universal Demo Rules (All 5 Leads)

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Start the demo within 30 seconds of joining | Don't waste time on small talk beyond "thanks for joining." |
| Show a REAL vacancy (or realistic one) | Not dummy data. They need to see themselves in it. |
| Let them see the Hebrew AI output | The "wow" moment is a good post appearing in 3 seconds. |
| Show the posting queue actually working | Copy → Open → Mark. The speed is the second "wow." |
| End with results tracking | The data story is what makes them say "I need this." |
| Always close with a specific next step | Never end with "let me know what you think." |
| Keep the demo under 10 minutes | They agreed to 5. Going to 10 is ok. Going to 15 loses them. |
